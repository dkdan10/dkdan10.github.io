FADEAWAY

Thirty NBA seasons of shots on one halfcourt that never moves.

Live Code Paper (PDF)

What it is

The artwork. FADEAWAY draws the NBA's public shot record, about 5.9 million regular-season attempts over thirty seasons, on one halfcourt that never moves. Every mark is a real record, and the image degrades exactly where the record does: where the archive places a shot coarsely, or refuses to place it at all, the piece draws that rather than smoothing it over. For 89 days, from October 27, 2015 to January 23, 2016, the league's SportVU optical tracking was public. That window is the only period where the motion between a shot's endpoints survives.

The pipeline. Python over 5.9M shot attempts across 30 seasons and 632 tracked games at 25 Hz: it mirrors the public archives, measures each season, joins every tracking game to the shot log, and writes compact binary artifacts. A zero-dependency TypeScript/Canvas renderer draws everything from those artifacts, and render(mode, t) is a pure function of one time value, so scrubbing backwards rebuilds the frame and never remembers.

The gate. A bare run reproduces a fixed acceptance table, decodes every shipped binary, and runs a catalogue of 121 seeded defects, each of which must be caught by the named check that owns it; a catch by an unrelated check fails the suite. One zone function is checked from Python and from TypeScript against one shared fixture. A purity probe proves that scrubbing backwards rebuilds and never remembers. The acceptance table is a self-measured regression fixture: it catches the pipeline changing, not the record being wrong.

Three things worth asking me about

  1. The record's idea of "where" is not constant. In 1996–97 the shot log files 95.4% of rim shots on a single coordinate; from 2010–11 through 2013–14 none; then the pile returns at new addresses, dunks first, then tip-ins. The piece draws each refusal as a pin at the coordinate the record actually used. Corroborated by a second instrument for exactly one season (2015–16); the other five cannot be checked by anything public.
  2. Joining tracking to the shot log is subtle. Event numberings disagree in 101 of the 632 tracked games by a small constant, so every shot silently takes its neighbour's coordinate. Each game's offset is calibrated by letting its releases vote, then checked by geometry. Held-out validation: 99.0% for straight joins, 84.6% for accepted shifts. The join recovers about 45% of shots; five detectors were built and scored before accepting that as the ceiling.
  3. The gate. A bare run reproduces the acceptance table, decodes every shipped binary, and runs the 121 seeded defects, each caught by the check that owns it and by no other. The defects corrupt data and documents, not code. The rule behind it: a gate that cannot be shown going red is not evidence.

The paper

CONTESTED: How Data Is Defined, Gathered, and Owned

Daniel Keinan · Written for "Theories, Methods, and Experiments in Art and AI" (Columbia University, Dr. Emily Spratt) · August 2026 · 14 pages, PDF

An NBA statistic is made the way an artwork is: someone chooses the instant, the subject, and where the definitions are drawn. The league publishes the picture while withholding the choices. The paper follows those choices through home-scorer bias in the box score, the three incompatible official meanings of "contested," the 89-day public tracking window and its closure, the Sportradar/Second Spectrum and Hawk-Eye agreements, player data rights under the NBA and NFL collective bargaining agreements, and Refik Anadol's Living Memory: Messi. FADEAWAY is the paper's evidence in material form.
